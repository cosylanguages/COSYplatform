# Greek can-do proposals, A1 and A2

Twenty-seven lessons whose can-do names a form instead of a task. Nothing here is applied: the
curriculum notes and the decks still carry the left-hand column. This file exists so a Greek speaker
can approve, correct or reject each line before anything changes.

**Why I am not applying it myself.** I cannot check my own Greek. A can-do that reads plausibly but
is wrong is worse than a drill, because it looks reviewed. Everything below is written to the
pattern the rest of the catalogue now follows - `Μπορώ να` + subjunctive - with simple, A1/A2
vocabulary, but it needs a native reader.

**What a reviewer should check, per line:**

1. Does `Μπορώ να …` take the right verb form here (present subjunctive for habitual, aorist for a
   single event)?
2. Is the sentence natural Greek rather than translated English?
3. Is it at the right level - would an A1 learner really do this in week one, an A2 learner in the
   second year?
4. Does the lesson's own content support it? Where a lesson has only vocabulary and drills, the
   proposal describes what those drills are *for*; if the lesson cannot support it, say so and the
   lesson needs content, not a can-do.

## What applying it would take

Three edits per lesson, the same shape as the Italian, English and French work already in this
branch:

1. the `cando:` line inside the lesson's `teacher_notes` in `curriculums/el/general/A1.json` or
   `A2.json`;
2. `canDo` in the deck;
3. the two strings the deck copies the can-do into - the objective line on slide 1 and
   `Ελεύθερη παραγωγή:` on slide 7.

`build_decks.py` regenerates decks from the manual pages and would do 2 and 3 automatically, but it
needs the COSYmanuals checkout, which this workspace does not have.

## A second question this file raises

Read the left-hand column straight through and the shape is obvious: articles, then the present, then
the subjunctive, then the past, then the passive. **The Greek courses are grammar syllabuses.** That
is a legitimate design, but it is not the design French B1 was re-cut to, where themes lead and
grammar is threaded through them. Which one is the house style is a decision above this file; if the
answer is theme-led, these 27 can-dos are the wrong repair and the courses need re-cutting instead.
