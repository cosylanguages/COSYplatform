# A2 Adult Speaking Assessment System

This assessment system turns the 11 planned A2 assessment entries into a monolingual, speaking-led checkpoint system for adult learners. It is designed for the existing 15-unit General English A2 course and can be used with the 60 authored lessons without changing any lesson IDs.

## 1. Practical A2 speaking rubric

Score each learner on four criteria using a 1–4 scale. Maximum score: 16. A2 pass threshold: 10–12 points, with no criterion below 2.

| Criterion | 4 — Strong A2 (ready for B1) | 3 — Solid A2 (target pass) | 2 — Developing A2 (borderline) | 1 — Below A2 (A1 level) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Task completion & interaction | Completes every part of the task; asks natural follow-up questions; reacts to the partner and keeps the exchange moving. | Completes the main task; answers directly; asks at least one follow-up question or reacts once. | Answers basic questions briefly; waits for the partner or assessor to lead; finds it hard to sustain a two-way exchange. | Gives one-word or memorised answers; does not respond to the partner; task remains incomplete. |
| Grammar control | Uses present, past, and future forms accurately most of the time; self-corrects minor slips without losing flow. | Controls Present Simple/Continuous and basic Past Simple; attempts future forms and comparatives; errors rarely block meaning. | Relies heavily on Present Simple even for past or future events; frequent verb or word-order errors make some ideas unclear. | Uses isolated words or fixed phrases; lacks basic sentence structure across time frames. |
| Vocabulary & functional chunks | Uses topic-specific adult collocations and paraphrases unknown words clearly in English. | Has enough everyday vocabulary for familiar adult topics; uses taught speaking frames. | Repeats basic words; pauses often when searching for everyday words; struggles to paraphrase. | Vocabulary is limited to basic survival words; cannot express opinions or plans. |
| Fluency, pronunciation & monolingual independence | Speaks in connected phrases with natural pauses; clear pronunciation; uses English strategies when stuck; zero L1 use. | Speaks with noticeable planning pauses but keeps a steady pace; mostly clear pronunciation; stays in English. | Frequent long pauses and restarts; pronunciation sometimes requires listener effort; may look for translation. | Very slow, fragmented speech; heavy pauses between words; relies on non-English words or gestures. |

## 2. Mid-course checkpoint (after Unit 8 / midterm entry)

**Timing:** 8–10 minutes per pair, plus 2 minutes of silent preparation.

**Assessed scope:** Units 1–8.

- Present Simple vs Present Continuous
- Question forms
- do vs make
- Present Perfect form and use
- used to
- Past Simple and Past Perfect
- Present Perfect vs Past Simple
- Second Conditional
- Defining relative clauses
- have to / must / mustn't
- Comparatives and superlatives
- too / enough

**Format:** paired interactive tasks with assessor observation.

### Part A — Routine contrast (3 minutes)

Assessor prompt:

> Tell your partner about your typical work or study week, and one thing that is different this week. Partner, listen and ask two follow-up questions.

Target evidence: Present Simple plus frequency adverbs vs Present Continuous; have to; active listening questions.

### Part B — The changed plan (4 minutes, pair role-play)

**Learner A card**

You planned to meet your friend or colleague at a cafe near the central station today, but your morning went wrong. Apologise and explain what happened earlier (you missed a bus, got stuck in traffic, or were working on an urgent task when something happened). Suggest a different cafe or neighbourhood spot near a park or metro stop and give simple directions. Ask if the new time and place work for them.

**Learner B card**

You are waiting near the central station. Your friend or colleague calls to change the plan. React naturally and ask one question about what happened. Check two details about the new place (noise level, seating, Wi-Fi, or distance from the station). Agree on a final meeting time and place.

### Part C — Mini-story exchange (2–3 minutes)

Assessor prompt:

> Think about a trip, a commute, or a day that did not go as planned, or something you used to do differently a few years ago. Tell your partner for about one minute. Partner, react and ask one question.

Target evidence: Past Simple, Past Continuous, used to, sequence linkers, reactive phrases.

## 3. End-of-course checkpoint (after Unit 15 / final exam entry)

**Timing:** 10–12 minutes per pair, plus 2 minutes of silent preparation.

**Assessed scope:** the full A2 course. This adds quantifiers, comparatives and superlatives, going to / will, First Conditional, should / must / have to, Present Perfect vs Past Simple, and relative clauses.

### Part A — Experience-to-detail interview (3–4 minutes)

Assessor prompt:

> Ask each other about two life or work experiences using "Have you ever...?" For example: living in another place, changing your routine, trying a new skill, or having an unusual trip. When your partner says "Yes", ask "When?", "Where?", or "What was it like?"

Target evidence: a smooth switch from Present Perfect to Past Simple.

### Part B — Comparing options and giving advice (4–5 minutes, pair decision)

Shared scenario card:

> A colleague wants to improve their health, save money, and manage their time better this year. Compare these three options:
>
> 1. Joining a 24-hour gym near the office (expensive, modern equipment, very crowded after 6:00 PM).
> 2. Cooking meals at home on Sundays and running outdoors (cheap, saves weekday time, depends on the weather).
> 3. Taking an online fitness and nutrition programme (flexible schedule, can be done anywhere, easy to lose motivation).

Task: compare the options (cost, convenience, time, results), discuss what happens if the colleague chooses each one, agree on the best option, and give two practical pieces of advice using should / shouldn't / don't have to.

### Part C — Define and guess (2 minutes)

Purpose: check that learners can explain unknown words in English without translating.

Each learner receives two everyday adult words or concepts and defines them using who, which, that, or where while the partner guesses.

- Set A: a co-working space / a deadline
- Set B: a commuter / a receipt

## 4. Administration guidelines

- **Zero-translation rule.** If a learner forgets a word, prompt in English: "How can you say that in another way?" or "Is it a person, a place, or a thing?" Reward successful paraphrasing under Criterion 3.
- **Balanced pairing.** Pair learners of similar speaking confidence so one learner does not dominate the role-play.
- **Actionable feedback.** Give two concrete English takeaways per learner, for example: "Strength: you switched smoothly from Present Perfect to Past Simple. Next step: use going to instead of Present Simple for next week's plans."
- **Assessor role.** Do not join the role-play unless the pair is stuck. Note evidence quietly and give delayed feedback.
- **Record keeping.** Record the four criterion scores, the total, and the two takeaways in the learner's progress record.
