| Item | Detail |

## Pedagogical pillars

- Monolingual from day one. Teachers use visuals, timelines, realia, gestures, contrast pairs, and high-frequency frames. Translation is not part of the lesson design.
- Speaking first. Every lesson moves from context and noticing to controlled practice and then to a real speaking task.
- Adult relevance. Topics include work, hybrid routines, home, food, shopping, city travel, digital habits, health, and personal goals.
- Spiral progression. Vocabulary and grammar return in later lessons so learners build automaticity.
- Four skills in every lesson. Reading, listening, writing, and speaking all appear in the 10-slide sequence.

## CEFR A0–A1 can-do descriptors

By the end of the course, learners can:

- Spoken interaction: start and keep simple face-to-face conversations about work, routines, free time, prices, directions, and plans. Ask for repetition, spelling, or slower speech.
- Spoken production: describe living spaces, daily habits, past weekends or trips, and short-term plans with connected phrases and basic time markers.
- Listening: understand slow, clear everyday speech, prices, clock times, schedules, and short voice messages when the context supports them.
- Reading: find facts in short practical texts such as profile cards, home listings, menus, transit notes, chat threads, and personal updates.
- Writing: write short functional texts such as contact cards, workspace notes, habit summaries, shopping lists, past-event posts, and sequenced plans.

## Assessment policy

- Continuous assessment: every lesson ends with two strengths and one concrete target.
- Mid-course checkpoint: end of A0 Unit 4. Covers introductions, objects, rooms, time, routines, frequency, likes, and basic questions.
- A0 exit check: end of A0 Unit 9 after the review unit. Covers the full A0 range.
- End-of-course checkpoint: end of A1 Unit 3 after the review unit. Covers present routines, past experiences, future plans, transactions, and collaborative decisions.
- Scoring: 16-point speaking rubric across task completion, grammar, vocabulary, and pronunciation.
- Certificate: 11/16 or higher and at least 80% attendance.

The full rubric and checkpoint tasks are in `teacher-guides/assessment-tools/a0-a1-speaking-rubric.md`.

## Classroom guidelines

- English-only zone. Learners use survival phrases such as “Can you repeat that?” and “What does this mean?” instead of switching to their first language.
- Pair rotation. Group learners change partners every 10 to 15 minutes so they hear different voices and accents.
- Micro-practice between lessons. Learners complete one short writing or voice-note task after each lesson.
- Error correction. Correct the target language during controlled practice. During fluency tasks, note errors and give delayed feedback.
- Inclusion. Offer large print, extra thinking time, and choice of roles. Avoid forcing personal disclosure.
