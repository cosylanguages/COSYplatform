# A0–A1 speaking assessment pack

This pack gives teachers one consistent way to assess speaking across the General English A0/A1 course. It contains a 16-point rubric, a mid-course checkpoint, and an end-of-course checkpoint. All tasks are monolingual and use the language already taught in the lessons.

## When to assess

| Checkpoint | When | Lessons covered |
|---|---|---|
| Mid-course | End of A0 Unit 4 | `my-daily-routine`, `who-does-it`, `a-man-of-100-faces`, `temperament-matters` |
| A0 exit | End of A0 Unit 9, after the review unit | `live-long`, `remarkable-people`, `how-i-met-my-friend`, `what-a-record`, plus `inspired-01` to `inspired-04` |
| End of course | End of A1 Unit 3, after the review unit | A1 core lessons plus `inspired-05` to `inspired-08` |

## 16-point speaking rubric

Score each criterion from 1 to 4. The total is out of 16.

| Criterion | 1 Emerging | 2 Developing | 3 Target | 4 Solid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Task completion and interaction | Uses isolated words or gestures. Needs heavy prompting. | Answers direct questions with short phrases. Rarely asks back. | Completes the task. Asks one or two simple follow-up questions. | Completes all parts naturally. Initiates, responds, and checks understanding. |
| Grammar and sentence frames | Uses memorised chunks only. No control of basic verb patterns. | Uses `be` and present simple in fixed frames. Frequent omissions. | Uses present simple, `there is/are`, and `can` with mostly accurate word order. Errors do not block meaning. | Contrasts present simple, past simple, and `going to` clearly in connected speech. |
| Vocabulary and everyday range | Knows a few survival words. Frequent breakdowns. | Covers personal information, objects, days, and times. Relies on pointing or single nouns. | Uses everyday words for work, routines, city places, food, and prices with some pauses. | Combines topic vocabulary flexibly across routines, directions, shopping, past events, and plans. |
| Pronunciation and fluency | Very slow and fragmented. Difficult to follow. | Understandable in short bursts. Long pauses. Flat rhythm. | Generally clear despite first-language accent. Pauses between phrases, not mid-word. | Speaks in clear chunks with intelligible stress and `-s` / `-ed` endings. Recovers smoothly. |

## Score guide

| Total | Placement | Next focus |
|---|---|---|
| 4–6 | A0 foundation | Survival frames, `be`, numbers, spelling, short scripted exchanges. |
| 7–10 | A0+ to A1- mid-course | Lessons 5–8. Routines, preferences, simple schedules. |
| 11–13 | A1 pass | Meets the A1 speaking exit standard. Ready for A1+ or A2. |
| 14–16 | Strong A1 / A2 ready | High fluency across present, past, and future frames. |

## Mid-course checkpoint

Format: paired live assessment, about 6 to 7 minutes per pair.

### Part 1: warm-up and personal check (1.5 minutes)

Ask each learner three questions:

1. What do you do, and where do you work or study?
2. What time do you usually wake up on weekdays?
3. What do you like doing in your free time?

### Part 2: paired information gap, “The new coworking space and schedule” (3 minutes)

Give each learner a half-completed profile card for a remote worker. Learner A has Samir. Learner B has Elena. The cards show workspace items, daily schedule, and habits.

The learners ask three or four questions to fill the gaps:

- What time does Elena start work?
- Is there a coffee machine in Samir’s office?
- How often does she work from home?

Then they compare the two profiles and decide who has a busier schedule.

### Part 3: real-life micro-chat, “Finding a time to meet” (2 minutes)

Give both learners a three-day mini-calendar for Wednesday, Thursday, and Friday. Each day has two busy blocks.

The learners talk together to find one free time for a coffee or a quick call. They confirm the day and time and say goodbye politely.

## End-of-course checkpoint

Format: paired live assessment, about 8 to 10 minutes per pair.

### Part 1: real-world transaction role-play (3 minutes)

Each pair draws one scenario card and acts it out without preparation notes.

Scenario A: cafe and city directions. Learner A is a visitor. They order a drink or snack and ask how to get to the train station or museum. Learner B is the barista. They give prices, take the order, and give simple directions.

Scenario B: weekend plans and market budget. Learner A and B plan a small dinner. They check what food they have, decide what to buy within a 20 euro budget, and agree on a meeting time.

### Part 2: “Now, then, next” mini-monologue and partner questions (4 minutes)

Give each learner a three-box visual prompt card:

- Now: my routine
- Then: last weekend
- Next: next month

Learner A speaks for 60 to 90 seconds. They cover one current habit, one thing they did last weekend or last week, and one plan for next month. Learner B listens and asks two or three follow-up questions.

Switch roles.

### Part 3: collaborative decision task (2 minutes)

Show a visual menu of three weekend or evening options, for example a street food market, an outdoor cinema, and a cooking workshop. Each option has a price, a time, and a short icon.

The learners discuss what they like and do not like, point out problems, and agree on one option together.

## Examiner delivery

- Keep prompts visual and concrete. Do not explain grammar rules during the test.
- If a learner freezes, use a visual cue or an easier parallel prompt.
- Score in real time with a four-column tick sheet.
- Give 30-second feedback: one strength and one concrete target.

Example feedback:

- Strength: “You asked great follow-up questions and your directions were very clear.”
- Target: “After ‘yesterday’, use the past verb. Say ‘I went’ and ‘I had’, not ‘I go’ and ‘I have’.”
